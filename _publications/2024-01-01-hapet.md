Encountered-type haptic displays provide direct feedback by positioning a tangible surface at the point of interaction. However, prior systems have largely focused on flat surfaces, while virtual pet research has primarily relied on visual feedback and 2D screen-based haptics. HaPet addresses these gaps with a hemispherical, desktop-level encountered-type haptic display that presents a dynamic curved surface for direct, free exploration in VR.

To evaluate its effectiveness and usability, we developed a virtual pet application and compared dynamic HaPet with a static HaPet and the VIVE controller. The study examined immersion and enjoyment during virtual pet interaction. Results show that HaPet's dynamic haptic feedback significantly enhanced both measures compared with the static HaPet and the VIVE controller, demonstrating the value of shape-changing, visuo-haptic feedback for direct interaction with virtual pets.
